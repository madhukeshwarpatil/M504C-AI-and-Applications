Explanatory Data Analysis for a US Multi-City Retailer¶

M504 AI and Applications (individual). Consultant EDA of retail_data.csv (Kaggle Retail Data; Khan, n.d.).

1. Business context¶

Northline Retail is a hypothetical multi-city US retailer. It asked for EDA of a 200,000-row extract covering ten cities (14 July 2020–14 July 2023): recorded profit, discounts, marketing, competition, operations and customer spend. The client needs an evidence-based assessment of which observed customer, pricing and operational patterns can support commercial decisions, while identifying limitations in the extract.

The file is a public Kaggle extract, not audited accounts (Khan, n.d.).

2. Dataset overview¶

Each row is one purchase by a unique CustomerID (no repeat customer). Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/abdurraziq01/retail-data (accessed 17 September 2026). Column names were cross-checked against dfasure (n.d.); that source is not used for results.

5. Data preprocessing¶

Source CSV is not overwritten. Parse PurchaseDate; add RecordedMargin, YearMonth, PriceGap, PriceGapPct and DiscountInconsistent; use city as the location unit; keep all rows (no imputation or de-duplication).

6. Business questions¶

Eight questions. Observational language only. “Recorded profit” is the file field. All calculations use the full 200,000 rows. Scatterplots use df.sample(2000, random_state=42) only to reduce overplotting.

Business importance. Assortment needs independent category economics. If recorded margin only restates discount, this extract cannot guide space or ranging.

7. Final discussion and conclusion¶

Strengths. Reproducible from retail_data.csv. Complete keys; questions could return null or mechanical results.

Limitations. One row per customer. StoreID is not a store. No campaign calendar. Causal use is limited (§3).

Overarching findings.

  1. Profit field limitation. Recorded profit equals list price × discount %. Rankings on that field are discount rankings.
  2. Weak commercial differentiation. Categories and cities are almost equal on recorded margin, price and volume.
  3. Weak operational associations. Marketing, foot traffic, inventory, income and competitive position do not track recorded profit.
  4. Structure constrains decisions. There is no repeat customer, no valid store key and no campaign grain, so CRM, store league tables and marketing ROI cannot be read from this extract.

Business recommendations

  1. Do not reallocate space or city investment on these tiny gaps (findings 2, 4).
  2. Do not target high-income customers as high spenders from the supplied score (finding 3).
  3. Do not claim marketing, traffic or inventory as recorded-profit levers from this file (finding 3).
  4. Treat ±10% vs competitor as a descriptive IQR band, not a recorded-profit rule (finding 3).
  5. Report year-on-year on full months only; flag Jul 2020 and Jul 2023 (calendar coverage).

Data-quality recommendations

  1. Replace recorded profit with price minus independently measured COGS before judging markdowns or assortment (finding 1).
  2. Map each store key to one location before any local attribution (finding 4).
  3. Investigate zero-inventory sales and inconsistent discount flags (finding 4).
  4. Capture campaign-level marketing and a true on-hand inventory/traffic grain before ROI tests (findings 3, 4).
